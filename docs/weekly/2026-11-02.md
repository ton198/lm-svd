# Weekly report: 2026-10-27 to 2026-11-02

All numbers come from `data/experiment_results/` and the CIRC Slurm logs. **No NeuralSVD run has converged** (`converged=false`). Different evaluations load different snapshots of the same run, so each table lists the training step it used. Every comparison below describes those snapshots only.

## Summary

1. **exp2 now scores the joint probability** $\log p(h,f\mid\text{BOS})$ instead of $\log p(f\mid h)$. After row centering, the two scores give the same spectrum. The change matters only for uncentered and `deflate1` analyses, and mostly for task data.
2. **Paper LinGen (exp14).** We reproduced the paper's coefficient-fitting and generation method at a smaller scale. Finite-table coefficients work best. Single-token coefficients are statistically tied with them on Small. NeuralSVD coefficients are worse, and they collapse when the basis is nonsense text.
3. **Our lingen.** This generates directly from the NeuralSVD reconstruction $u(h)^\top W(f)\tilde E^\top$. exp6 is clearly the best architecture, but its samples are still poor text. Diagnostics (exp15) put most of the error in the right (future) function. Retraining only that function (exp16) cuts exp6's autoregressive KL from 0.82 to 0.54.

## 1. exp2: conditional score replaced by the joint score

### What changed

| | Before (≤ 10/27) | Now (`9123809`, 10/28) |
|---|---|---|
| Matrix entry $B[h,f]$ | $\sum_t \log p(f_t \mid h, f_{<t})$ | $\log p(h + f \mid \text{BOS}) = \sum_t \log p(h_t \mid \text{BOS}, h_{<t}) + \sum_t \log p(f_t \mid \text{BOS}, h, f_{<t})$ |
| Context start | history with no prefix | GPT-2 `<\|endoftext\|>` (50256) as BOS, even for mid-document windows |
| Metadata | — | records `bos_token_id` and the score definition; there is no fallback if the model has no BOS |

- **Compute:** inference and analysis moved to the CIRC `h100` partition (`521ffdd`).
- **Fit weighting:** every log-log spectrum fit, exp2 included, now weights each point by its log-index cell width (`d06e6db`). Before this change, the dense tail dominated the fit.

Algebraically, the new score equals the old one (with BOS in front of the history) plus a per-row constant $\log p(h\mid\text{BOS})$. That constant is a rank-1 term $a\mathbf 1^\top$.

### Results: before and after (GPT-2 Small; both use the weighted fit)

| Data | Score | Slope, none | $E_1$, none | Slope, deflate1 | $E_{10}$ / $E_{50}$ after deflate1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| WikiText (512×512) | conditional | −1.15 | 99.76% | −0.69 | 63.5% / 85.5% |
| | **joint** | **−1.27** | **99.97%** | −0.69 | 64.5% / 86.1% |
| Task (1309×1309) | conditional | −1.30 | 99.99% | −0.81 | 78.2% / 90.9% |
| | **joint** | **−1.38** | 99.98% | **−0.93** | **94.8% / 97.9%** |

- **Row centering gives the same answer for both scores.** The slopes are −0.906 and −0.904 on WikiText, and −1.142 and −1.137 on task data, with matching $E_1$ and $E_{10}$. Row centering removes the added $\log p(h)$ term exactly, so the joint score adds no new structure beyond that per-history term.
- On WikiText, every history has 64 tokens and the change is small. On task data, histories are instructions of varying length (up to 256 tokens), so $\log p(h)$ varies widely across rows. That is likely why the energy after deflate1 becomes much more concentrated (78% → 95% at $k=10$). We have not verified this explanation.

## 2. Paper LinGen (exp14)

### What it does

This follows Golowich, Liu and Shetty (2025), Algorithm 1. The idea is to write a **target** history $t$ as a linear combination of **basis** histories $h_1,\dots,h_N$, so that $\text{logits}(t) \approx \sum_i v_i\,\text{logits}(h_i)$. The same coefficients $v$ are then reused while generating text.

**Data** (`wikitext-large`, 64-token histories, 32-token futures): 2,048 basis histories, 50 target histories, and 1,024 futures for the finite table. Coefficients are **always fitted with GPT-2 Small**.

**Step 1: fit one coefficient vector $v\in\mathbb R^{2048}$ per target.** We use three methods:

| Method | What is regressed | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Finite** | Centered logits of $h_i + f_j$ on 50 header tokens per future. The header tokens are GPT-2's top 50 next tokens after $f_j$ alone. The target row is built the same way. `lstsq`. | 2,048 × 51,200 |
| **NeuralSVD** | Uses the exp6 left features $u(\cdot)$ (snapshot at step 110.2k). It minimizes $(u(t) - \sum_i v_i u(h_i))^\top G\,(u(t) - \sum_i v_i u(h_i))$, where $G = \mathbb E_f[W(f)\tilde E^\top \tilde E W(f)^\top]$ is averaged over all 10,000 dataset futures and the full vocabulary. This is the same least-squares fit as Finite, but on the learned operator and over every future and token. | 256-dim feature space |
| **Single-token** | Full-vocabulary centered next-token logits at the end of $h_i$, with **no future**. | 2,048 × 50,257 |

Each method has two options:
- `opt1` uses the real basis histories and futures.
- `opt2` ("nonsense") shuffles the tokens within the basis histories and within the futures. The targets stay real.

**Step 2: generate.** At step $s$, with generated tokens $z_{<s}$:
- $q(\cdot) = \text{softmax}\big(\sum_i v_i\,\text{logits}_M(h_i + z_{<s})\big)$. The **evaluation model $M$ runs a forward pass on all 2,048 basis histories** with the generated tokens appended.
- $p(\cdot) = \text{softmax}(\text{logits}_M(t + z_{<s}))$, which is the true continuation of the target.
- Sample $z_s \sim q$ at temperature 1, for 15 steps, with 5 generations per target.
- $M$ = GPT-2 Small tests the method within one model. $M$ = GPT-2 Large tests whether the coefficients fitted on Small transfer.

**Baseline:** short context, where $q$ is $M$ applied to only the last 5 tokens.
**Metric:** KL(p‖q) over the full vocabulary, where $p$ is the evaluation model $M$ and $q$ is the method, both on the same trajectory. It is averaged over generations at each position and **summed over 15 positions**. Lower means closer to $M$.

**Differences from the paper:** we use 2,048 bases instead of 40,000, 1,024 futures instead of 10,000, and fixed 64/32-token windows instead of variable 8–30-token windows.

### Results: KL(p‖q) against the evaluation model, summed over 15 tokens (top-1 agreement with $M$ in parentheses)

| Eval model / basis | Finite | NeuralSVD | Single-token | Short context (5 tok) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small / real | **6.89** (0.63) | 9.66 (0.54) | 7.06 (0.60) | 17.46 (0.46) |
| Small / nonsense | **9.21** (0.55) | 30.25 (0.31) | 12.82 (0.47) | — |
| Large / real | **11.76** (0.55) | 14.66 (0.50) | 13.07 (0.51) | 21.46 (0.43) |
| Large / nonsense | **16.16** (0.43) | 39.49 (0.20) | 22.61 (0.32) | — |

Every number above is KL(p‖q) against the **evaluation model itself**: $p$ is $M$ reading the target plus the generated tokens. No method is compared against another method directly.

The table below gives the **difference between two such KL values**, $\Delta = \text{KL}(p\,\|\,q_\text{method}) - \text{KL}(p\,\|\,q_\text{Finite})$, with a 95% bootstrap CI over the 50 targets. A positive value means the method is further from $M$ than Finite is.

| | NeuralSVD − Finite | Single − Finite | Short − Finite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small / real | +2.77 [2.03, 3.56] | +0.17 [−0.57, 0.94] | +10.57 [9.33, 11.82] |
| Small / nonsense | +21.04 [12.95, 33.09] | +3.61 [2.03, 5.78] | — |
| Large / real | +2.90 [2.15, 3.68] | +1.31 [0.28, 2.38] | +9.70 [8.14, 11.23] |
| Large / nonsense | +23.33 [14.24, 36.51] | +6.45 [4.47, 9.10] | — |

The CI covers only resampling of targets. It does not include refitting or seed variation.

- **All linear methods beat a 5-token context** by about 10 nats. The basis-combination idea works at this scale.
- **Finite ≈ Single on Small.** Adding futures to the regression does not help within the same model. Futures do help for transfer to Large (+1.31, and the CI excludes 0) and with nonsense bases.
- **NeuralSVD coefficients are about 2.8 nats worse with real bases, and collapse with nonsense bases** (logit error 0.62–0.69, compared with 0.12–0.19 for Finite). exp6 was trained only on real text, so its features probably do not represent shuffled-token histories. Here NeuralSVD changes only $v$: generation still runs the real LM on the bases.
- **Transfer to Large** adds about 5 nats for every method and keeps the ranking unchanged.

## 3. Our lingen: generating from the NeuralSVD reconstruction

### What it does

There is **no LM forward pass on any basis**. Each next-token distribution comes from the learned features alone (the exp6 feature extractors run frozen GPT-2 on $h$ and on $f$ separately, but never on $h+f$):

$$\hat\ell(h,f) = \Big(\sum_k u_k(h)\,W_k(f)\Big)\tilde E^\top,\qquad q = \text{softmax}(\hat\ell),\qquad p = \text{softmax}(\text{GPT-2 logits at the end of } h{+}f)$$

Here $u$ is the learned left (history) function, $W$ is the learned right (future) function, and $\tilde E$ is the vocabulary-centered unembedding. The singular values are already folded into the features.

- **onestep:** sample 4,096 independent $(h, f)$ window pairs ($H=64$, $F=32$) and compare $p$ and $q$ once per pair.
- **autoregressive:** 64 sequences × 96 steps. At each step, sample $z \sim q$, drop the oldest token of the 96-token window, append $z$, and split the window back into $h$ (64 tokens) and $f$ (32 tokens). $p$ is evaluated on the **same** window. A separate free run of GPT-2 provides a perplexity reference.
- **Metrics:** KL(p‖q) **averaged** per pair or step, top-k overlap, the squared logit error $\|A-\hat A\|^2/\|A\|^2$, and the GPT-2 perplexity of the generated tokens.

| | Paper LinGen (exp14) | Our lingen |
|---|---|---|
| Source of $q$'s logits | $\sum_i v_i\,\text{logits}_M(h_i + z)$, with a real LM on 2,048 bases | $u(h)^\top W(f)\tilde E^\top$, with learned features only |
| What is learned | $v$ per target, fitted once | the feature functions $u, W$ (NeuralSVD training) |
| Context during generation | fixed target, with tokens appended | sliding 96-token window |
| KL reported as | **sum** over 15 positions | **mean** per pair or step |

**KL values cannot be compared between the two sections.**

- Train and validation numbers agree to within about 0.15 KL, so we see **no overfitting** to the training stream.
- **exp6 is the only feature-based architecture that is close.** Frozen GPT-2 inputs matter (exp6 vs exp8, same MLP).
- **Distillation helps the causal student** (exp11 vs exp12 on every metric). exp13 (no skip) has finished training but has no lingen run yet.
- Even for exp6, GPT-2 assigns its samples a perplexity of about 1,400, compared with 85 for its own text. The reconstruction is good on average, but sampling from it drifts.

### Where the error comes from (exp15, probe KL)

On the fixed training probe, we refit one side at a time by least squares and keep the other side fixed:

| Snapshot | Rank oracle (exact SVD) | Keep learned left, refit right | Keep learned right, refit left | Original |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| exp6 | 0.027 | **0.070** | 0.962 | 0.971 |
| exp9 | 0.027 | 0.076 | 3.438 | 3.459 |
| exp11 | 0.068 | 0.082 | 1.259 | 1.266 |

- The learned **left span is close to sufficient**: refitting the right side alone recovers almost the oracle KL.
- Refitting the left side alone changes almost nothing, so **the error sits in the right (future) function**. This is descriptive and holds on the probe only. The probe refit of the right side is a per-future lookup table, not a function that generalizes.
- Scale and temperature calibration change little.
